# 1. The textbook: what each video teaches

Read at: stage 1 (reading the chapter and the brief), stage 2 (the plan), and whenever a scene is added or changed.

You are making one episode of a Bangla educational YouTube series for school science (Bangladesh NCTB curriculum, classes 6 to 10). Every episode follows the same structure, look and rules, so students recognise the format. Only the topic content changes. Follow every rule in these files; where two rules seem to conflict, ask me.

## Turn the textbook's static pictures into motion

The main purpose of the series is to show, as animation, what the textbook can only show as a still figure.

- Before planning, go through the chapter and list every figure, diagram and activity (চিত্র and কাজ) that belongs to this topic.
- Each of them must appear in the video as a moving version: the ray travels, the object falls, the magnet pulls, the needle turns.
- Keep the same arrangement and viewing angle as the textbook figure, so a student can match the video to the book page.
- In the plan, include a table: textbook figure or activity number, what it shows, which scene animates it. A figure may be left out only if I approve it.
- If you cannot read the textbook pages, say so and ask me to attach them. Do not guess what the figures contain.

## Stay inside the textbook for that class

- Teach only what the textbook chapter for that class teaches, at the depth it teaches it, using its terms and definitions.
- Do not add explanations from higher classes. Example of what went wrong in video 1: explaining light with electrons and waves is too advanced for class six, where the book stops at light travelling in straight lines, reflection and absorption.
- Simple everyday analogies are welcome when they explain a textbook idea. A new scientific concept that is not in the chapter is not.
- If you think one idea beyond the textbook is necessary, propose it in the plan as a separate, clearly marked item and wait for my approval. The default is to leave it out.

## Splitting a chapter into videos

- At the start of a new chapter, also propose how the chapter is split into videos (my rule, 2026-11-26). There is no fixed number per chapter: split by content, so that each video is about 10 minutes and every textbook figure and activity gets about a minute or more. Keep lessons in book order and do not split one lesson across two videos. For example, chapter 9 (10 lessons) is two videos (lessons 1-3 and 4-10: first planned as three, merged on 2026-11-28), chapter 10 (12 lessons) is three, chapter 11 (12 lessons) is two (lessons 1-3 and 4-12); a short chapter may need one or two, a long one four. When merging lessons keeps every figure at about a minute, a video may run to about 12 minutes and about 1,400 words (chapter 11 video 2: 11 min 25 s, 1,343 words). I approve the split with the plan.

## Changes from the textbook

- A textbook person may be moved to another place to fit a scene (Anusheh at the bus stand was), but list every such change in the plan and in the delivery note for approval.
- A rickshaw or a cycle cannot be shown without drawing its rider. Use a closed vehicle (car, bus, train with dark windows), say in the narration what the book uses, and ask for approval in the plan.
- Every change from the textbook (a person moved, an object replaced or left out) is listed in the plan and in the delivery note, and is approved by me.
