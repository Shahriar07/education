# 4. What may be drawn, and drawing real things realistically

Read at: stage 2 (the plan: what replaces a person, tree or animal), stage 4 (building every object), stage 5 (checking frames).

## Never drawn (strict)

- Never draw a complete living creature: no human, animal, bird, fish or insect, and no face, head or human-shaped shadow or silhouette.
- A separate hand, finger or eye may be drawn when needed (a hand lighting a candle, an eye at the end of a light ray). Hands have their own rules in 05-hands.md.
- A skull or a bone may be shown (2026-11-25: it is not a living being). In chapter 11 the jaw, foot and arm are shown as bones.
- Do not draw trees, plants or crop fields. A fruit such as a mango is allowed. Sky, sun, moon, stars, water, buildings and objects are allowed.
- When a textbook figure shows a person or trees, keep the arrangement of the figure but replace the person with a single eye or a hand, and trees with a lamp post (the chapter 10 book itself lists the lamp post as a thing at rest).
- An animal named in the book (a firefly among the bright objects) is named in the narration and shown by what it does (a small light blinking in the dark), never drawn.

## Draw real things realistically

- Everyday objects must look like the real thing in shape, proportion, colour and shading. A student should recognise the object instantly without a label.
- Whatever a label or sign names must be there: a shop sign "ফল ঘর" needs fruit in the shop, a "বই ঘর" books, a "ফার্মেসি" medicine boxes, a tea stall a kettle and glasses. Draw goods per shop type (`shopGoods` in world3d.js).
- The scene matches its label: a label "গ্রামের পথ" needs a village, not a city. Show true size next to known things (a house, a road).
- Example of what went wrong in video 1: the mango was a flat yellow blob and did not look real. A mango needs its true shape (fuller at the shoulder, a slight beak near the tip), a colour gradient (green to yellow with a red-orange blush), a highlight, a shadow and a short stem.
- Before using an object, look at a frame of it by itself and ask whether it would pass for that object. If not, redraw it. Check mentally against how the real object looks in Bangladesh (a kolshi, a tin roof, a rickshaw, a hurricane lamp).
- Things with states show the state clearly: on a switchboard, off = every rocker alike; on = the pressed one flipped.
- Diagram elements (rays, arrows, labels) stay simple and clean. Realism applies to the objects, not to the diagram marks.
- Everything must move: rays travel, objects slide in, numbers count up. No static slides, except title and recap cards.
